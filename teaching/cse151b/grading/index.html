Many components are scored on a four-level scale: NP (no pass), LP (low pass), P (pass), and HP (high pass). Full details are in the syllabus.

Components

Pre-lecture homeworks (NP / LP / P / HP)

One homework per class session, on PrairieLearn, based on the pre-lecture materials. Graded for both completeness and correctness, with unlimited attempts for full credit.

  • Completeness: attempt every question before the start of lecture.
  • Correctness: you may revisit and resubmit through Friday night; your grade is your PrairieLearn score at the deadline.

Programming assignments — PAs (NP / LP / P / HP)

Based on your PrairieLearn submission and confirmed by your PA oral assessment: you earn the grade from your submission score only if you pass the oral assessment.

PA oral assessments (pass / no pass)

For each PA, a member of the teaching staff will ask you to:

  • Understand: explain a portion of your code.
  • Predict: say what would happen if a specified change were made.

You may retake the assessment if you do not pass the first time. If you do not pass a second time, you will meet with course staff to create an improvement plan, and the PA grade is decided case by case. (Inspired by the DUE method from Adam Blank's course at Caltech: readings.caltech.codes/ta-howtos/dues.)

Exams (0–100)

Taken in the CBTF, with an opportunity to retake. Exams relate most closely to the pre-lecture homework material, and the PAs and pre-lecture homeworks are designed to reinforce the same content.

Standards: course letter grades

The course uses standards-based grading (reference reading from Prof. Joe Politz, link to be finalized). Your letter grade comes from three components: engagement & social learning, assignments, and exams. The three lowest pre-lecture homework scores are dropped before applying these standards.

A

  • Above 80% on both exams
  • HP on at least 3 of 4 assignments, P on the remaining one
  • HP on all 4 Learning Process & Resource Reflections
  • Completed 80% of pre-lecture homeworks for completeness, before class
  • HP on 80% of pre-lecture homeworks for correctness, P on the rest
  • HP on the project

A+: HP on all components. A−: meeting the B standard in exactly one non-exam category (a lower exam score moves you to the B range).

B

  • Above 80% on one exam, above 70% on the other
  • HP on at least 2 of 4 assignments, P on the other two
  • Completed 60% of pre-lecture homeworks for completeness, before class
  • HP on 60% of pre-lecture homeworks for correctness, P on the rest
  • HP on all 4 Learning Process & Resource Reflections
  • HP or P on the project

B+: achieving the A standard in any category. B−: meeting the C standard in exactly one non-exam category (a lower exam score moves you to the C range).

C

  • Above 60% on one exam, above 50% on the other
  • P on at least 3 assignments
  • Completed 60% of pre-lecture homeworks for completeness, before class
  • P on at least 60% of pre-lecture homeworks for correctness
  • P on all 4 Learning Process & Resource Reflections
  • P or LP on the project

C+: achieving the A or B standard in any category. C−: missing exactly one non-exam category (a lower exam score moves you to F or Incomplete).

No D grades are assigned; below the C standard receives an F or Incomplete. Please talk to the teaching staff at any point if you feel you are not likely to pass — we are here to help.

Late days & exceptions

Late time is counted hourly. No more than one late day (24 hours) is allowed per submission; nothing later than 24 hours after the deadline is graded, and a missing submission 24 hours past the deadline receives a 0.

  • Assignments: 48 late hours (2 late days) total for the course, max 12 late hours per assignment — so assignments are due Thursday at noon at the latest, to keep you on track for the Thursday oral assessment.
  • Pre-lecture homeworks (completeness): due before class each day; 3 lowest dropped.
  • Pre-lecture homeworks (correctness): 48 late hours total for the course, max 1 late day each; since these are due Friday, the latest is Saturday night.
  • Project: 24 late hours (1 late day) total across the proposal and the final submission.

You must sign up for oral exams by a date to be finalized before the exam. If you have extenuating circumstances needing more accommodation than the standard late days, please reach out to the instructor.
